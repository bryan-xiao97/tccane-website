Embedded Interest Form and Personal Google Sheets Access functional spec

Date: August 6, 2026

Sources used: user-provided AGENTS.md instructions and confirmed planning choices; index.html; app.js; styles.css; functions/api/interest.js; functions/lib/handler.js; functions/lib/validate.js; functions/lib/turnstile.js; functions/lib/rate-limit.js; functions/lib/google-token.js; functions/lib/sheets.js; functions/lib/dlq.js; functions/scheduled/retry-dlq.js; README.md; wrangler.toml; .dev.vars.example; package.json; relevant tests; Google Sheets Interest Submission Storage - Functional Spec - 08.05; current official Google OAuth, Google Sheets, Google Drive and Cloudflare documentation.

Chosen approach: Add a simple inline form to the existing “Get involved” section. Keep the current server-side submission and reliability boundary, replace service-account authentication with one-time personal-owner OAuth using per-file drive.file access and require an active scheduled retry Worker. A completed write or durable queue produces one persistent visitor confirmation.

Goal

Purpose

Give visitors a simple on-site way to express interest while removing the shared service-account dependency. The form and the personal Google Sheets authorization change are equally important parts of one reliable submission workflow.

Success criteria

  • A visitor can enter first name, last name and email, complete abuse verification and submit without leaving the website.
  • A valid submission that is immediately written or durably queued transitions to a clear persistent confirmation panel.
  • Each completed submission produces one five-column row in a spreadsheet owned by the designated personal Google account, with practical deduplication for ambiguous delivery failures.
  • The production write path contains no service-account credential and requires no spreadsheet sharing with a service-account identity.
  • The Google authorization grant permits access only to the app-created spreadsheet rather than every spreadsheet in the owner’s account.
  • Validation, rate limiting, Turnstile and durable retry behavior remain active.
  • Unrecoverable configuration or OAuth failures never produce a false success message.
  • Queued submissions are processed by an actively scheduled retry runner rather than depending on manual replay.

Non-goals

  • CRM integration or volunteer registration in a third-party system.
  • Dashboards, spreadsheet reporting, downstream automation or staff workflow tooling.
  • Visitor Google sign-in or visitor access to the destination spreadsheet.
  • A production administrator interface for choosing sheets or renewing access.
  • A second fallback datastore.
  • Turning Google Sheets into a broader or permanent system of record.
  • Redesigning unrelated website sections.

Hard constraints

  • The site remains vanilla HTML, CSS and JavaScript hosted on Cloudflare Pages.
  • The public write boundary remains the same-origin POST /api/interest Pages Function.
  • Google client credentials, refresh tokens and access tokens remain server-side and are never exposed to a visitor’s browser.
  • The destination spreadsheet is owned by the designated personal Google account.
  • Google access uses the supported OAuth library and the non-sensitive per-file drive.file scope.
  • The spreadsheet is created during one-time local owner setup so the per-file grant applies without Google Picker or broad Sheets access.
  • Turnstile, IP rate limiting, opaque submission identifiers, RAW Sheets writes and DLQ retention remain part of the workflow.
  • A scheduled Cloudflare Worker is part of the finished capability because Pages Functions do not provide the required scheduled retry execution.

At stake: Whether the visitor receives success when the system cannot write or establish a credible retry path.

Desired end-state

Visitor experience

  1. The visitor reaches the existing “Get involved” section and sees labeled first-name, last-name and email fields, concise contact-use context, Turnstile and one submit control. Existing contact and social links remain secondary.
  2. Browser validation catches empty or malformed fields without replacing server validation.
  3. During submission, the control clearly indicates progress and prevents duplicate clicks.
  4. The page sends the established JSON payload, including the honeypot value and current Turnstile response, to the same-origin endpoint.
  5. An immediate write or durable queue replaces the form with one persistent, accessible success panel.
  6. A validation, rate-limit, verification, network or permanent authorization failure leaves the form available, retains appropriate visitor-entered values and presents actionable error copy without claiming success.
  7. After a failed request consumes or expires its Turnstile token, the visitor receives a fresh verification state before retrying.

Owner setup and authorization

  1. The owner runs the setup locally and signs into the designated personal Google account.
  2. The setup requests offline drive.file access and creates one spreadsheet owned by that account.
  3. The setup creates the fixed Submissions table with headers submissionId, submittedAtUtc, firstName, lastName and email.
  4. The deployment stores the OAuth client ID, client secret, refresh token and spreadsheet ID in Cloudflare secrets. None are committed or returned to the browser.
  5. The OAuth application is configured for durable production authorization rather than a seven-day Testing grant.
  6. No service-account secret remains active and the spreadsheet is not shared with a service-account email.

Submission and retry behavior

  1. The existing handler continues to validate the payload, enforce rate limits and verify Turnstile before creating a submission.
  2. The backend obtains access tokens from the owner’s stored refresh token and calls the existing Sheets REST write path.
  3. The row remains limited to the opaque submission ID, UTC timestamp, first name, last name and normalized email. RAW interpretation prevents form values from being evaluated as formulas.
  4. A confirmed append returns recorded. A retryable destination failure that is successfully stored in the DLQ returns accepted. The front end treats both as completion.
  5. The scheduled Worker processes due DLQ records with the same OAuth grant, spreadsheet configuration and deduplication behavior.
  6. A revoked or invalid owner grant produces visible submission failure until the owner repeats authorization. It does not produce visitor success.

Explicitly out of scope after completion

The finished capability does not include CRM registration, reporting, dashboards, spreadsheet automation, visitor Google identity, a production owner console, arbitrary spreadsheet selection, a fallback database or changes to unrelated site content.

Assumptions & open questions

  • Assumption: The designated personal Google account can create and own the destination spreadsheet and can authorize the project’s OAuth client.
  • Assumption: One spreadsheet and one fixed Submissions tab are sufficient for the expected operating period.
  • Assumption: The existing five-column row schema and current public request contract remain acceptable because they are necessary for the confirmed reliability behavior.
  • Assumption: Exact field labels, confirmation copy and failure copy will follow the current site voice while meeting the behaviors specified here.
  • Open question: The exact personal Google account, final spreadsheet title and production Turnstile site key are deployment values and have not been named in this spec.
  • Open question: No response-time or recovery-time service level was specified. The required scheduled retry Worker establishes active retry, but its final cadence remains an operating configuration.
  • Known ownership trade-off: Personal ownership removes the shared service account as requested but makes the owner’s continued account access and OAuth grant operational dependencies.
